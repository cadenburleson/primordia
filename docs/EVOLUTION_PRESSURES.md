# Environmental experiments and the anatomical boundary

The guide suggests experiments, not a proven recipe for the fastest evolution of complex life. Its buttons select existing tools; they do not award functions, edit genes or pick winners. The nursery retains the normal cellular rules in a smaller habitat. Generation throughput, survival and inherited function are different measurements.

## Questions to try

| Question | Intervention | Useful evidence | Failure to watch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Can connected descendants persist? | Keep a reproducing population below the cell ceiling; preserve attached descendants | Continued division and inherited material functions | Bigger clusters without useful function or viable offspring |
| Does movement help find food? | Paint separated fuel patches; cautiously reduce light only if intake is light dominated | Food acquisition and displacement under matched actuation-on/off trials | Gravity, fluid drift or growth mistaken for propulsion; starvation |
| Does protection help survival? | Bring saved, distinct lineages together near food; use an observed consumer if one exists | Prey-derived intake, blocked damage, losses and surviving offspring | Assuming a placed body is a predator without measured feeding |
| Do changing seasons retain alternatives? | Prepare 50% variation and a 960-cycle year; apply explicitly | Reproduction and persistence through multiple parts of a cycle | Short harsh cycles causing extinction instead of adaptation |

The numerical settings are exploratory starting points, not calibrated biological time scales. The guide does not run experiments for the user. Changes in temperature alter chemistry and light changes energy intake; they do not specifically mutate cells toward a desired function. Saved snapshots and matched comparisons matter more than visual novelty.

## Primary research behind the design

- [Herron et al., 2019 — De novo origins of multicellularity in response to predation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-39558-8): two of five predator-selected algal populations developed simple multicellular forms in roughly 750 asexual generations. Larger forms resisted the experimental filter feeder. This motivates testing an actual survival benefit. Our contact-digestion model does not reproduce that predator's size threshold, so the same outcome is not predicted here.
- [Bergland et al., 2014 — Genomic evidence of seasonal adaptive oscillations in Drosophila](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4222749/): repeated seasonal changes in variant frequencies were consistent with varying selection maintaining genetic alternatives. This motivates adjustable environmental cycles, not an assumption that seasons inevitably generate more complex anatomy.
- [Shubin et al., 2006 — The pectoral fin of Tiktaalik and the origin of the tetrapod limb](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature04637): the fossil appendage combined fin structure with joints and substrate-supported postures. The engineering lesson is to enable useful, heritable intermediate structures and physical interactions before expecting walking. The fossil is not a simulation recipe or a guaranteed linear progression.

## What the engine can represent today

Genes regulate a designed vocabulary of material production and cell behavior. Attached growth, polarity, mineral support, contractile polymers, joint mechanics, signaling, digestion, membranes and transport already interact with resource and energy stores. Shape comes from cellular growth and physical contacts. These ingredients do not establish that organs, coordinated walking or intelligence have evolved from ordinary founders.

A true flagellum requires more than turning up movement pressure. The current model has no subcellular flagellar filament/rotor. A horn can resemble a protruding arrangement of cells, but dedicated tip-contact injury and horn construction are not modeled. Arms/legs need a repeatable developmental arrangement with coordinated, useful locomotion; that outcome has not been demonstrated in the autonomous founder population.

The next anatomical engine work would therefore need:

1. Heritable, material-costed filament construction and motor control, with fluid coupling and equal/opposite momentum transfer. No free thrust from a named trait.
2. Developmental control of repeated protrusions and attachment sites, with growth, repair, breakage and energy costs.
3. Shape-aware contact mechanics and injury, so a protrusion can actually act defensively and incur a tradeoff.
4. Evidence of useful locomotion, survival and reproduction across descendants and matched mechanism-removal trials.

These are remaining capabilities, not additions claimed by this UI revision. No physical experiments or performance benchmarks were run during this change at the user's request; compiled artifacts and small fake-world controller checks do not validate evolutionary outcomes.
