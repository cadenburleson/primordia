# Early Earth and the road life took

Research for Primordia, gathered 2026-10-11, and what it means for the engine. Dates in Ga are billions of years ago, Ma millions. "PAL" is the present level of oxygen in the air. Dates from molecular clocks depend on the model used; points the sources disagree on are marked contested.

## Part 1. How life arose and diversified

### Where it began (no consensus)

- **Alkaline vents.** Hydrogen-rich alkaline fluid meets a carbon-dioxide-rich, more acid sea across thin iron-sulfide walls. That gives a proton gradient of the size and direction living cells still use. It needs a sea with no oxygen, hydrogen, carbon dioxide and metal sulfides. How the carbon was first reduced is unknown. ([Sojo 2016](https://marineinfo.org/doc/publication/329236))
- **RNA first.** Well supported as a stage, but nobody has shown the first nucleotides or a self-copying RNA forming unaided. ([Martin 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4390851))
- **Warm ponds and hot springs.** Wetting and drying joins small molecules into long ones. It needs exposed land, fresh water and a supply of organics. ([Pearce 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5664559), [Damer & Deamer 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7133448))

### The first cell we can reconstruct

- The last common ancestor of everything alive lived about 4.2 Ga, with around 2,600 proteins. It needed no oxygen, built itself from hydrogen and carbon dioxide (making acetate), had an early immune system, and so already lived among other things. ([Moody 2024](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10195667/))
- An earlier reconstruction adds: fixed its own nitrogen, lived hot. Whether it lived hot is contested. ([Weiss 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/nmicrobiol.2016.116), [Catchpole & Forterre 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6878951/))

### Ways of making a living, in the order they appeared

| When | Way of living | Takes in | Gives off | Pays |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ~4.2 Ga | Acetate-making | Hydrogen, carbon dioxide | Acetate | About 0.3 ATP per acetate |
| by ~3.5 Ga | Methane-making | Hydrogen, carbon dioxide | Methane | Under 0.5 ATP per methane |
| early | Fermenting what others leave | Dead matter | Acids, alcohol | 2 ATP per sugar |
| by 3.4 Ga | Light without oxygen | Light plus sulfide, hydrogen or dissolved iron | Sulfur or rust; no oxygen | |
| ~3.47 Ga | Sulfate-breathing | Sulfate, hydrogen | Sulfide | Beats methane-makers to the hydrogen |
| by 3.2 Ga | Fixing nitrogen from the air | Nitrogen gas, much energy | Usable nitrogen | Oxygen destroys the enzyme |
| 3.4–2.5 Ga (contested) | Light with oxygen | Light, water, carbon dioxide | Oxygen | Needs nothing scarce |
| 3.2–2.8 Ga | Breathing oxygen | Food, oxygen | Carbon dioxide | 30–32 ATP per sugar, up to 15 times fermenting |
| ~2.5–2.3 Ga | Nitrate-breathing | Food, nitrate | Nitrogen gas | About 60% of oxygen's yield |

Sources: [BioNumbers](https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=112841), [Lemaire 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2020.00486/full), [Hanada 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4791109/), [Shen 2001](https://www.nature.com/articles/35065071), [Stüeken 2015](https://vpl.uw.edu/publication/isotopic-evidence-for-biological-nitrogen-fixation-by-mo-nitrogenase-from-3-2-gyr), [MIT 2021](https://news.mit.edu/2021/photosynthesis-evolution-origins-0928), [Strohm 2007](https://www.iab.kit.edu/zumft/Abstracts/Abstr123.html).

### Oxygen was a poison first

- Between 2.46 and 2.06 Ga oxygen in the air rose from about 0.001% to as much as 10% of today's. ([Great Oxidation Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Oxidation_Event))
- Oxygen breaks the metal centres of the very enzymes early life ran on: those that handle hydrogen, fix nitrogen and make methane. ([Lu & Imlay 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9191689))
- Defences had to be made: enzymes that disarm oxygen's reactive forms. Life that never made them survives where oxygen does not reach: sediment, vents, guts.
- A nitrogen-fixer that also makes oxygen needs a special cell. In filaments about one cell in ten gives up harvesting light, thickens its wall and fixes nitrogen for its neighbours, who feed it. ([Heterocyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterocyst))

### Mats: the first ecosystems

- Layered mats a few millimetres thick are the oldest widely accepted fossils, 3.43–3.48 Ga. ([Allwood 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41586-018-0610-4))
- Each layer lives on its neighbour's waste. On top, oxygen-makers in the light. Below them, light-users that need sulfide rising from beneath. At the dark base, fermenters, methane-makers and sulfate-breathers. ([Ellsworth 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12779409/))

### Genes passed sideways

- Between 1.6% and 33% of a microbe's genes arrived recently from another lineage, and over time about four fifths of genes have moved this way. ([Boto 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2842723))

### Complex cells

- A complex cell is a merger: an archaeon took in a bacterium that became the mitochondrion, some time between 2.05 and 1.2 Ga. Fossils of complex cells date from about 1.65 Ga. ([Tobiasson 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12338589/), [Betts 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6152910))
- Why it mattered: thousands of internal power stations give a cell vastly more energy for each gene it runs, enough to be large and complicated (contested in detail). A cell that swallows prey without them would have to eat about 34 times its weight for each division. ([Martin 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5584316))
- Sex was already present in the ancestor of all complex cells. ([Malik 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2488364))
- Algae began the same way: a complex cell swallowed a light-harvesting bacterium and kept it, about 1.9 Ga. ([Sánchez-Baracaldo 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5604047))

### Bodies, animals and land

- Many-celled life arose at least 25 separate times. Oldest fossil 1.63 Ga. ([Miao 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10807817/))
- In the laboratory, a single-celled alga exposed to a predator that swallows small prey formed eight-cell colonies too big to swallow in under 100 generations. ([Herron 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41598-019-39558-8))
- The first large animals (571–541 Ma) were mostly fixed in place and absorbed their food. From 538.8 Ma, within 13–25 million years, came predators, eyes, shells and burrowing. Flesh-eaters are scarce wherever oxygen is low. ([Sperling 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3746845))
- Land was taken in order: microbial crusts (3.22 Ga), then fungi and plants (about 470–425 Ma), then arthropods, then four-legged animals (about 390–375 Ma). Plants needed a waxy skin, pores, tough spores and stiffened vessels. An ozone layer like today's forms once oxygen reaches about 1% of the present level. ([Homann 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41561-018-0190-9), [ozone models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.10557))

### Numbers worth keeping

- A bacterium is about 1 cubic micrometre; a yeast cell about 42. ([BioNumbers](https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=106307))
- A microbe makes about 0.003 copying errors per genome each time it divides. ([BioNumbers](https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=106788))
- Living matter holds carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus at about 106:16:1. ([Redfield ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redfield_ratio))
- About a tenth of what one level of a food chain makes reaches the next. ([Ecological efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_efficiency))
- Among bacteria, bigger cells live faster for their size; among complex single cells, metabolism rises in step with size; in animals it rises with size to the three-quarters power. ([DeLong 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2919978))

### The ladder, with what triggered each step

1. ~4.2 Ga. Living on hydrogen and carbon dioxide at vents. Needs no oxygen.
2. By ~3.5 Ga. Methane-making. Same inputs.
3. Fermenters. Need the dead matter the first cells left.
4. By 3.4 Ga. Light without oxygen. Needs light plus sulfide or dissolved iron.
5. ~3.47 Ga. Sulfate-breathing. Makes the sulfide the light-users need.
6. ~3.4 Ga. Layered mats. Each layer uses its neighbour's waste.
7. By 3.2 Ga. Nitrogen fixing. Usable nitrogen was scarce.
8. 3.4–2.5 Ga. Light with oxygen. Needs only light and water.
9. 3.2–2.8 Ga. Oxygen-breathing, wherever oxygen collected.
10. 2.46–2.06 Ga. Oxygen fills the air. The old life retreats; defences are needed.
11. ~2.5–2.3 Ga. Nitrate-breathing. Needs nitrate, which needs oxygen.
12. ~2.05–1.2 Ga. The mitochondrion: one cell living inside another.
13. By ~1.8–1.65 Ga. Nucleus, swallowing prey, sex.
14. ~1.9–1.3 Ga. Algae: a complex cell keeps a light-harvester.
15. 1.63–1.05 Ga. Simple bodies, under pressure from predators that swallow small prey.
16. 575–539 Ma. Large animals, once oxygen allowed.
17. From 538.8 Ma. Predators, eyes, armour.
18. ~470–375 Ma. Land.

## Part 2. How the young planet worked

### Timeline

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| 4.4 Ga | Liquid water and crust already, from a single ancient crystal ([Wilde 2001](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v409y2001i6817d10.1038_35051550.html)) |
| 4.2–3.4 Ga | Heavy bombardment, now thought prolonged and not one late spike (contested) ([Zellner 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06694)) |
| 2.5 Ga | Land above the sea probably 2–3% of the surface, against 27% now ([Charnay 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06265)) |
| 2.43–2.22 Ga | Oxygen first appears in the air, comes and goes for 200 million years, then stays ([Poulton 2021](https://www.leeds.ac.uk/news/article/4797/)) |
| 2.45–2.22 Ga | Three ice ages, at least one reaching the tropics ([Kopp 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1183582/)) |
| 1.8–0.8 Ga | A billion years in which little changed |
| 717–635 Ma | Two more global ice ages ([Rooney 2015](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1473458)) |
| 850–540 Ma | Oxygen rises toward today's level |

### The air

- Before oxygen: nitrogen much as now; carbon dioxide 10 to 2,500 times today's; methane 100 to 10,000 times; hydrogen at tens of parts in a million; oxygen under a millionth of today's. ([Catling & Zahnle 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7043912))
- When methane passes about a tenth of the carbon dioxide, an orange haze forms and cools the planet by up to 20 degrees.
- An ozone shield needs oxygen at 1% of today's by older models and 5–10% by newer ones (contested). ([Cooke 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11675))

### The Sun and the thermostat

- The Sun was 26% dimmer at 4.0 Ga, 21% at 3.0, 15% at 2.0 and 8% at 1.0. ([Feulner 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.4449))
- Carbon dioxide and methane kept the seas liquid. A thousandth of an atmosphere of methane adds about 14 degrees.
- Rock weathers faster when it is warm and the air holds more carbon dioxide, and weathering locks carbon dioxide away as limestone. Volcanoes return it. Over about a million years the two balance, which held the early Earth between roughly 0 and 50 °C. ([Krissansen-Totton 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5910859))
- When oxygen destroyed the methane, about 10 degrees of warming went with it, and the ice came.

### The sea

- Slightly acid at first (pH about 6.6), neutral by 2.5 Ga, 8.2 today.
- Full of dissolved iron and almost empty of sulfate (under 2.5 millionths of a mole per litre, against 28 thousandths today). ([Crowe 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25378621))
- Close to saturated with silica, because nothing alive took it out. ([Conley 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2017.00397/full))
- Wherever iron-rich deep water met oxygen or iron-using light-harvesters, the iron fell out as rust. Those layers, laid down from 3.8 to 1.85 Ga, are the world's iron ore.
- Phosphate at 10–25% of today's, because there was little land to weather and iron rust carries phosphate down with it. ([Bjerrum & Canfield 2002](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v417y2002i6885d10.1038_417159a.html))

### Ultraviolet

- With no ozone, damage to DNA in surface water was about 1,000 times today's. Thirty metres down it matched today's sea surface. ([Cockell 2000](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/ultraviolet-radiation-and-the-photobiology-of-earths-early-oceans))
- Life near the top survived under mineral grains, in mats, and by moving down.

### Food and energy for the first life

- Hot acid vents: over 400 °C, rich in sulfide and iron. Cooler alkaline vents: 40–91 °C, pH 9–11, rich in hydrogen and methane.
- Lightning fixed nitrogen, perhaps a hundred times less once carbon dioxide fell (contested).
- Before any cell split water for its hydrogen, all life was limited by what the rock supplied: hydrogen first, then dissolved iron, then sulfur. The living world made at most a fortieth of what today's sea makes. ([Kharecha 2005](https://vpl.uw.edu/publication/a-coupled-atmosphere-ecosystem-model-of-the-early-archean-earth-geobiology-2005-2), [Ward 2019](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/5p2e2-mmn05))

### Moon and tides

- At 3.2 Ga the Moon was at 70% of its present distance and a day lasted about 13 hours. ([Eulenfeld & Heubeck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05464))
- Tides at 3.9 Ga were about 3.4 times today's, with a high tide every 6.8 hours. ([Davies 2021](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-1582.html))

### The layered sea

- A tenth of what the lit surface makes sinks out of it, and a hundredth reaches the bed. That strips the surface of nutrients; life at the top depends on deep water being brought back up. ([Sarmiento 2004](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v427y2004i6969d10.1038_nature02127.html))

### Cause and effect, for the engine

1. A brighter star warms the planet; warmth speeds weathering; weathering takes carbon dioxide out; the temperature holds.
2. Oxygen rises; methane is destroyed; the greenhouse fails; ice.
3. No oxygen, no ozone: life stays deep or under cover.
4. Deep water without oxygen fills with dissolved iron; where it wells up into light or oxygen it rusts out.
5. Nothing uses silica, so the sea is full of it.
6. Until a cell splits water, life is capped by the rock's supply of hydrogen and iron.
7. Little land, little phosphorus; more land, more.
8. Sinking remains starve the surface unless deep water returns.
9. A closer Moon means shorter days and bigger, more frequent tides.

## Part 3. Where the engine stands against this, and the build order

### What already matches

- Matter and energy are conserved: carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, iron, salt, silicon and water all have books that balance.
- Free oxygen starts at nothing, is made only by light-harvesters, and is soaked up by dissolved iron first.
- Genes pass sideways between touching cells. New genes arise by copying errors.
- A hunter can swallow a light-harvester and keep it: the step that made algae.
- Sex, bodies, cell fates, walls, shells, holdfasts, predators and prey all exist as ordinary genes.
- The sea has depth: light fades, vents feed only the bed, cells swim and are stirred in three dimensions.

### What does not

| On Earth | In the engine now |
| --- | --- |
| The first cells lived on hydrogen and carbon dioxide at vents, in the dark | The first cell harvests light and gives off oxygen from its first moment |
| Hydrogen, sulfide and methane were real substances that cells made and used | Vent chemistry is one number per site; there is no hydrogen, sulfide or methane |
| Five ways of living came before oxygen: acetate-making, methane-making, fermenting, light without oxygen, sulfate-breathing | One light gene (always makes oxygen), one vent gene, one for dissolved food, one for remains |
| Breathing oxygen pays up to 15 times what fermenting does | It pays twice |
| Oxygen poisoned life that had no defence | Oxygen only helps |
| Only lightning fixed nitrogen until cells learned to, and oxygen destroys the enzyme | No cell fixes nitrogen |
| A swallower without mitochondria cannot make a living | Every eater starves: the engine's oldest unsolved problem, and probably the same one |
| The young Sun was a quarter to a third dimmer; greenhouse gases and rock weathering held the temperature | Temperature is a setting. Nothing ties it to the star or the air |
| No ozone until oxygen: the surface and the land were bathed in ultraviolet | No ultraviolet |
| The Moon was close and tides were large | No tides |
| Surface water and deep water hold different things; sinking remains strip the surface of nutrients | Each column of sea is one well-mixed stock |

### Build order

Each stage ends with a measurement on several planets, not one.

**Stage 1. The chemistry of a world without oxygen.**
Hydrogen and sulfide come out of vents as real dissolved substances. Methane is a gas in the air. The vent gene becomes what it was on Earth: hydrogen plus carbon dioxide into living matter. New genes: methane-making, light without oxygen (it uses sulfide or dissolved iron and leaves sulfur or rust), sulfate-breathing, fermenting what others leave.
*Gate: a planet seeded only with the hydrogen-eater holds a living population at its vents with no light-harvester on it.*

**Stage 2. Oxygen as revolution and as poison.**
Without oxygen a cell gets about a fifteenth of the energy in its food. Breathing oxygen becomes a gene, as does a defence against it. Oxygen injures cells that have no defence. Nitrogen-fixing becomes a gene that oxygen switches off.
*Gate: when oxygen rises, undefended kinds fall back to the dark bed and defended breathers take the surface; eaters that breathe hold their own without restocking.*

**Stage 3. An Earth-like beginning.**
A way to start a world as Earth started: no oxygen, one kind of cell at the vents, everything else left to arise. The ladder of life's steps follows Earth's order.
*Gate: on some planets, light-harvesting and then oxygen arise unaided from the vent cell, and how long it takes is reported.*

**Stage 4. The young planet.**
Ultraviolet that water and, later, ozone shut out. A star that brightens with age, a greenhouse of carbon dioxide and methane, and rock weathering that draws carbon dioxide down faster when it is warm and delivers phosphorus and silica to the sea. Tides that wet and dry the shores.
*Gate: a planet whose oxygen rises loses its methane and cools; life is absent from the surface until ozone forms.*

**Stage 5. A layered sea.**
Surface and deep water keep separate stocks. Sinking remains carry nutrients down; upwelling brings them back.
*Gate: surface water runs short of nutrients away from upwelling, as it does on Earth.*

**Stage 6. Complex cells, bodies, animals, land.**
A swallowed oxygen-breather kept alive inside its host, as the mitochondrion was. Then the existing bodies, predators and land rules, retuned against the new chemistry.

## Progress

### Stage 1, first step: vents and hydrogen (2026-10-11)

Built:

- **Standing vent fields.** A planet is made with narrow lines of vents across its sea bed (about 250 of 3,200 sites on a small planet), each with a strength from nothing to one. Before this a planet had no vents at all until a random eruption made a hot spot.
- **Hydrogen as a real dissolved substance.** Vents pour it into the water over them; a tenth of what is dissolved leaks away each cycle. After 40 cycles the water over vents held 28 times what the open sea did.
- **The vent gene lives on it.** Hydrogen and carbon dioxide are joined into living matter, at a rate that matches a light-feeder under a bright star, and only on or near the bed. The planet's vents together supply far less than its star, so what limits vent life is the rock's supply, as it was on Earth.
- **The Archaeon starter** is set down at vents, born with its catalyst, and keeps the membrane transporter that sets how fast a cell can burn fuel.

Measured against the gate (*a planet seeded only with the hydrogen-eater holds a living population at its vents with no light-harvester on it*), on three planets with no restocking:

| Cycles | Cells |
| --- | --- |
| 300 | 999 to 1,156 |
| 600 | 200 to 238 |
| 900 | 84 and 89 (two planets run on) |
| 1,200 | 56 and 57 |

At 1,200 cycles 55 of 56 cells were on or beside a vent and 51 were gripping the bed. The early thousand lived on the planet's starting stock of dissolved food; the population then fell toward what the vents supply. It was still falling slowly at 1,200 cycles, so the gate is met only provisionally: the level it settles at has not been seen.

Not yet built in Stage 1: sulfide, methane, light without oxygen, sulfate-breathing, fermenting as its own gene.
